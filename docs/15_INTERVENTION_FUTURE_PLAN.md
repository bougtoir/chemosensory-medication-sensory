# 15 — Future Intervention Plan (DESIGN ONLY)

Designed only if H1–H4 survive. This phase performed NO intervention.

## Trial concept

**Question:** Can sensory conditioning alter medication-relevant sensory
response in genetically susceptible individuals?

**Design:** genotype-stratified randomized controlled trial.

- **Pre-intervention:** taste detection/recognition thresholds;
  response to a validated pharmaceutically-relevant bitter probe;
  pleasantness/aversiveness; acceptability of a safe surrogate stimulus.
- **Intervention:** dietary sensory training or controlled repeated
  exposure (duration anchored to adult-plasticity literature, ~4–12 weeks).
- **Post-intervention:** identical sensory battery.
- **Endpoint:** acceptability of a pharmaceutically relevant bitter
  stimulus or validated surrogate.

**Ethics constraint:** healthy participants are never exposed to
therapeutic drugs; surrogate bitterants only.

## Mediation model (primary)

```
Intervention → ΔSensory phenotype (S1) → ΔMedication-relevant acceptability (S2)
```

Genotype enters as a moderator of either arrow (analysis of
intervention × genotype interaction, pre-specified).

## Feasibility flags raised by Stage 1

- Bitter-specific hedonic plasticity in adults is the weakest arm of the
  evidence: bitter/sour liking did not shift with exposure (Mattes 1994),
  though detection thresholds and preferences for salt did shift
  (Bertino 1982/86). A trial should therefore power primarily on
  **threshold/intensity** endpoints, not hedonic endpoints.
- Consumption context matters: taste exposure without swallowing produced
  no salt-taste change (J Nutr 2023) — the intervention must include
  actual consumption, not rinsing.
