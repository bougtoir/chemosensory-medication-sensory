# 21 — Public registration summary

**Study.** ToMMo study 2025-0057: taste-bud-related gene polymorphisms and prescription change (TMM Community-Based and Three-Generation Cohorts, Miyagi, age ≥20, baseline and second-stage surveys).

**Theory.** Chemosensory phenotype S = f(G, E): a genetic component plus acquired plasticity. If the chemosensory interface contributes to drug-related behaviour M = g(S, D), inter-individual differences in medication patterns contain a component predicted by chemosensory genotype. This study tests only the population-level genetic part; it does not test plasticity, conditioning, response modification or any intervention.

**Primary hypothesis.** Pre-specified variants in 20 approved taste-related genes (TAS2R38 PAV/AVI, TAS2R9 V187A, TAS1R2 rs12033832) predict longitudinal medication change between surveys for drugs predicted to activate the corresponding receptor (propylthiouracil, thiamazole, ofloxacin; Holm α = 0.05).

**Endpoints.** Seven longitudinal endpoints (any change, active-ingredient change, within-class switch, addition, removal, standardized dose increase ≥1.25×, decrease ≤0.80×) and drug-specific change-away; structured Drug/KEDD IDs first.

**Secondary.** Formulation/oral-sensory gradient (conditional on QC gates), FFQ triangulation, gene-constrained SNP analysis, ML with nested CV, continuous calibration of 12 evidence-scored predictions.

**Controls.** 12 frozen negative controls (route, masking, receptor mismatch, behavioural); one requires an amendment and is not analysed.

**Success / refutation.** Pre-committed interpretation patterns A–E; any control failure precludes a mechanistic claim; nulls in adequately powered tests are reported as informative nulls.

**Not claimed.** Adherence, persistence, taste-driven switching, intervention efficacy, immune mediation, health-economic or global-health benefit.

**Timing.** Frozen and hashed before receipt of any participant-level data.
